EDITORIAL KARPATHY PAIRS — APPLY ALL 32 TO THE ARTICLE:

=== INTENSIFIERS (cut on sight — delete unless meaning collapses) ===
WRONG: This is actually quite important because really this is rather a good point.
RIGHT: This is important. This is a good point.

WRONG: It is, in fact, the most effective approach. Surely you can see that.
RIGHT: It is the most effective approach.

WRONG: The evidence is just not there yet. Of course, more research is needed.
RIGHT: The evidence is not there yet. More research is needed.

=== PASSIVE VOICE (make active: doer → verb → object) ===
WRONG: The supplement was taken by the patient every morning.
RIGHT: The patient took the supplement every morning.

WRONG: A blood panel can be run by your doctor to check your levels.
RIGHT: Your doctor can run a blood panel to check your levels.

=== THROAT-CLEARING OPENERS (delete the preamble) ===
WRONG: It is worth noting that supplements can cause liver damage.
RIGHT: Supplements can cause liver damage.

WRONG: It should be mentioned that the study was limited by sample size.
RIGHT: The study was limited by sample size.

WRONG: There is evidence to suggest that vitamin D helps with mood.
RIGHT: Vitamin D may help with mood.

WRONG: There are several reasons why people take supplements.
RIGHT: People take supplements for several reasons.

=== CONTRAST TEMPLATES (replace "not X, but Y" with direct Y) ===
WRONG: The question is not whether supplements are bad. It is whether the structure around them has grown too big.
RIGHT: The question is whether the structure around supplements has grown too big.

WRONG: The goal is not to stop taking supplements. The goal is to find a healthier balance.
RIGHT: The goal is to find a healthier balance with supplements.

WRONG: The emotional toll is not a sign of weakness. It is a sign that the habit has stopped serving its purpose.
RIGHT: The emotional toll means the habit has stopped serving its purpose.

WRONG: The point is not to suffer. It is to discover what works for you.
RIGHT: The point is to discover what works for you through direct experience.

WRONG: That is not a loss. It is permission to let go.
RIGHT: It is permission to let go of something you never needed.

=== CLINICAL REGISTER (write like a parent at coffee, not a journal article) ===
WRONG: Health anxiety is a known driver of supplement overuse.
RIGHT: Health anxiety can drive supplement overuse.

WRONG: Nature-based interventions demonstrate moderate-to-strong effects on depressive symptomatology.
RIGHT: Time outside helps about as much as entry-level talk therapy for depression.

=== ABSTRACT → CONCRETE (replace vague with specific) ===
WRONG: The intervention resulted in improved outcomes for participants.
RIGHT: 80% of participants completed the program. Their anxiety scores dropped by half.

WRONG: Many people experience difficulties with cessation.
RIGHT: Many people find it hard to stop.

=== NOMINALIZATION (verbs beat nouns — find the buried verb) ===
WRONG: The cessation of the habit led to an improvement in sleep quality.
RIGHT: When people stopped the habit, they slept better.

WRONG: There was a reduction in symptoms after the implementation of the program.
RIGHT: Symptoms dropped after the program started.

=== WEAK VERBS (find a sharper verb) ===
WRONG: The study has findings that are important for treatment decisions.
RIGHT: The study found results that change how doctors treat patients.

WRONG: The program is one that helps people reduce their use.
RIGHT: The program helps people reduce their use.

=== SENTENCE RHYTHM (vary length — short hits like a verdict) ===
WRONG: The person reads that vitamin D deficiency is linked to fatigue and starts taking a high dose every day and feels more in control of their energy and when they forget a dose they worry the fatigue will come back.
RIGHT: The person reads that vitamin D deficiency is linked to fatigue. They start taking a high dose every day. They feel more in control. But when they forget a dose, the fear returns.

=== REPEATED OPENERS (change the start of consecutive sentences) ===
WRONG: Supplements can help with specific deficiencies. Supplements are not a replacement for a healthy diet. Supplements should be taken under medical supervision.
RIGHT: Supplements can help with specific deficiencies. They are not a replacement for a healthy diet. A doctor should guide what you take.

=== EM DASHES (replace with periods or commas) ===
WRONG: There are three key factors — motivation, access, and support.
RIGHT: There are three key factors: motivation, access, and support.

=== BANNED PHRASES (replace with direct language) ===
WRONG: Let us delve into the landscape of supplement overuse.
RIGHT: Let us explore supplement overuse.

WRONG: The tapestry of factors that contribute to addiction is complex.
RIGHT: Many factors contribute to addiction, but a few stand out.

WRONG: A steady approach over time yields the best results.
RIGHT: A consistent approach over time works best.

=== ATTRIBUTION FRAMING (kill "we know that" — no middleman) ===
WRONG: We know that supplements affect people differently.
RIGHT: Supplements affect people differently.

WRONG: Research tells us that health anxiety plays a role.
RIGHT: Health anxiety plays a role.

=== READER ASSUMPTIONS (never assert what the reader feels/thinks/experiences) ===
WRONG: You sense that what you are doing is not working.
RIGHT: Some people sense that what they are doing is not working.

WRONG: You reach for supplements because you worry about your health.
RIGHT: Many people reach for supplements out of health worry.

=== BRIDGE TO CARE (sound human, not institutional) ===
WRONG: Our facility offers comprehensive treatment options for individuals struggling with supplement dependence.
RIGHT: If supplements are causing real distress, a conversation with a healthcare provider is a low-risk first step.

WRONG: We are here to help you on your journey to recovery.
RIGHT: A therapist who specializes in health anxiety can help untangle the pattern.

=== ENDINGS (land on an image or action, not a summary) ===
WRONG: In conclusion, supplements can be helpful but should be used carefully.
RIGHT: The goal is to move from driven to thoughtful. One bottle at a time.

=== NATIVE SPEAKER FRICTION (fix phrasing that sounds translated) ===
WRONG: The bottles are something that can be set aside.
RIGHT: The bottles can be set aside.

WRONG: There is a concern that supplements may be one that is overused.
RIGHT: Some supplements may be overused.

=== BULLET LIST LEAD-INS (set the list up before the bullets land) ===
WRONG: The outside can look the same. The inside is completely different.
* **Relief vs. regret:** If a relationship ends and someone feels relief...
RIGHT: The outside can look the same. The inside is completely different. A few telltale signs point in one direction or the other:
* **Relief vs. regret:** If a relationship ends and someone feels relief...

WRONG: Each one targets a different mechanism. CBT works on behavior. Psychodynamic works on attachment. REBT targets rigid beliefs.
* **CBT:** CBT focuses on the here and now...
RIGHT: Each type of therapy approaches the pattern from a slightly different angle, which matters less than finding the one that fits:
* **CBT:** CBT focuses on the here and now...

WRONG: The costs of staying disconnected add up over time. Loneliness grows. Trust erodes.
* **Chronic loneliness:** A person is surrounded but feels alone...
RIGHT: The costs of staying disconnected add up over time. They are easy to miss until they feel heavy:
* **Chronic loneliness:** A person is surrounded but feels alone...

Vary the lead-in formula — never start two in a row with the same phrase. Avoid "Here are some..." — instead embed the setup in the flow of the paragraph so the colon arrives naturally.

=== SOURCE-ATTRIBUTION ANCHOR TEXT (the link IS the claim, not a wrapper around it) ===
WRONG: Clinical evidence supports these [coping strategies](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.21156), and therapists commonly teach them.
RIGHT: Therapists commonly teach these [coping strategies](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.21156) as part of BPD treatment.

WRONG: The [evidence supports therapy](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.21156) as the primary treatment for BPD hallucinations.
RIGHT: [Therapy is the primary treatment](https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.21156) for hallucinations associated with BPD.

WRONG: Research confirms that [DBT reduces distress](https://doi.org/...).
RIGHT: [DBT reduces distress](https://doi.org/...) in people with this condition.

The rule: if you remove the link, the sentence should still state the claim. "Evidence supports X" without the link becomes "Evidence supports" — a dangling attribution. "X" without the link is a complete statement. The link anchor should be the claim itself.
