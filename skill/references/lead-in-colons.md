# Lead-In Colons for Bullet Lists — Editorial Pair #31

**The problem:** Bullet lists that start abruptly after a paragraph, with no bridge sentence telling the reader what the list is doing.

**The fix:** Before every bullet list, the preceding sentence should set up what the list will deliver and end with a colon.

**WRONG:**
```
The outside can look the same. The inside is completely different.

* Relief vs. regret: If a relationship ends...
```

**RIGHT:**
```
The outside can look the same. The inside is completely different. A few telltale signs point in one direction or the other:

* Relief vs. regret: If a relationship ends...
```

**More examples:**

WRONG: Each one targets a different mechanism. CBT works on behavior. Psychodynamic works on attachment.
* CBT: CBT focuses on the here and now...

RIGHT: Each type of therapy approaches the pattern from a slightly different angle, which matters less than finding the one that fits:
* CBT: CBT focuses on the here and now...

WRONG: The costs of staying disconnected add up over time. Loneliness grows. Trust erodes.
* Chronic loneliness: A person is surrounded but feels alone...

RIGHT: The costs of staying disconnected add up over time. They are easy to miss until they feel heavy:
* Chronic loneliness: A person is surrounded but feels alone...

**Rules:**
- Vary the lead-in formula — never start two in a row with the same phrase
- Avoid "Here are some..." — it signals "I'm about to give you a list" instead of flowing naturally
- The colon should arrive from the paragraph's own momentum, not feel tacked on
- Numbered steps (1. 2. 3.) also need lead-ins, but can use periods instead of colons since they're inherently sequential
